# Atlas search methodology and limits

Atlas discovers current public conversation around a researcher-entered market interest. It is not a survey, sentiment score, attention measure, causal model, thesis generator, or investment recommendation.

## Retrieval and comparison

Each search runs a bounded English-language baseline using Bluesky, `lemmy.world`, a public Mastodon hashtag timeline when a valid tag can be formed, six The Conversation expert-analysis feeds, Crossref's recent scholarly metadata, arXiv preprint metadata, and Manifold's public crowd-forecast search. For a non-U.S. language/community lens, Atlas additionally searches Bluesky, up to six configured Lemmy instances, the selected Mastodon instance(s), localized Stack Exchange sites when available, and headline metadata from twelve Global Voices editions. Crossref and arXiv are global research context, not country views or online discussion; only bibliographic/title metadata and source links are returned, and neither is sent to the discussion summarizer. Manifold results are restricted to open binary MANA play-money markets with positive recent volume and liquidity; they are displayed as forecast context with source links and are not included in the country/community summaries. The API is public and read-only, no login or key is used, and no cash-denominated contracts are retrieved. A play-money platform's market probability is not real-money price discovery, verified odds, a representative survey, or a national view; Atlas is limited to non-commercial use under the API's documented terms. The two added Lemmy servers, Sopuli and sh.itjust.works, expose live keyless search but are server/community views—not verified Finland or Canada resident samples. Federation may overlap and duplicates are flagged. Global Voices is reporting and The Conversation is expert analysis; neither is forum discussion or included in the AI discussion summary. Every source group is labeled with its exact query, time window, retrieval time, result count, and status. Search results are not written to visitor history; Crossref and arXiv have separately disclosed, volatile server-memory caches to honor provider request guidance.

The English baseline adds a `lang:en` filter to Bluesky. Lemmy uses the phrase as entered. Mastodon searches a mechanically generated hashtag, not arbitrary post text; untagged posts are missed. Atlas does not use Mastodon's full-text status search: the official API docs say status results depend on server configuration and authenticated search, so that endpoint is not a reliable no-login public source ([Mastodon search API](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/search/)). When configured, AI may translate the phrase for selected-language providers. Otherwise it uses the phrase as entered. The exact phrase sent to each provider is shown. The country selector offers the United States/English, South Korea/Korean, Japan/Japanese, Germany/German, France/French, Brazil/Portuguese, India/Hindi, and Spain/Spanish. These are query/community lenses, not geolocation. The English baseline is not a verified sample of U.S. residents.

Bluesky returns up to 25 indexed posts per query from seven days. Lemmy searches up to 20 recent posts per configured instance from seven days; federated posts may overlap. Mastodon queries up to three separate hashtag timelines per selected instance, at most 20 API items each, and filters posts older than 30 days; untagged discussion is outside its coverage. Some country-language lenses include community-focused instances, but those are not verified resident samples. Global Voices returns matching headline metadata only, up to five per edition over 30 days. The Conversation searches six public Atom feeds and returns up to five exact headline matches per edition over 30 days, after item-level rights and attribution checks. Crossref returns up to 12 title matches published within five years, with DOI and bibliographic metadata; it does not return abstracts, measure paper quality, or identify national perspectives. arXiv returns up to 12 title-matched preprint metadata records submitted within five years, without abstracts or author names; the API is cached for 24 hours and distinct requests are spaced at least three seconds apart per server instance. arXiv preprints may not be peer reviewed and provide no evidence of public opinion. Provider outages, partial edition failures, and rate limits are shown per source; failures are not shown as zero matches. Exact URL/text repeats and conservative near-copies are flagged; the near-copy check requires at least 120 characters and 90% shingle containment (five-token shingles, or four-character shingles for Han/Hiragana/Katakana text). It can miss paraphrases, translations, and copies in short excerpts, and can still misclassify boilerplate; it is a review cue, not semantic story clustering. Hacker News has been retired from visitor search pending content reuse review. Specialist Q&A and scheduled ingestion health do not feed the current search result.

## AI and privacy

When a visitor submits a search, Atlas automatically attempts translation and an AI summary if the operator has enabled Groq and configured a private API key. It sends the topic and at most 16 short, source-balanced excerpts; embedded links, email addresses, and @handles are removed from excerpts before submission. Citation URLs and attribution fields are not sent. Social excerpts with a detectable external link are excluded from AI evidence and remain visible in Sources with a provenance warning; a linked headline, preview, or promotion does not establish the poster's independent view. Bare domains are detected for common public suffixes, but hidden/shortened links and copied material without a detectable URL may not be identified. Before display, a conservative phrase-level topic screen omits records whose title and available excerpt lack a query-topic term, while preserving the raw provider count and the screened-out count; clear aliases are limited and synonym-only matches can be missed. Before AI selection, excerpts must also contain market/economic vocabulary in English, Korean, Japanese, German, French, Portuguese, Spanish, or Hindi. Unknown language labels are checked against all listed vocabularies. Tests include positive/negative language examples, off-topic market-language examples, and alias examples; these are code fixtures, not a live accuracy benchmark. Neither screen is a semantic relevance or truth classifier. There is no per-search AI opt-in. For a selected-language overview, provider records with an understood matching language code are eligible; explicitly mismatched labels are excluded. Missing labels and unresolved Lemmy IDs may use conservative script detection for Korean, Japanese, or Hindi only; other unknown-language records remain visible but are not summarized. Lemmy language IDs are resolved against that same instance's live `/api/v3/site` catalog, and unresolved IDs remain labeled as such. Social records with attribution are limited to one excerpt per displayed account per provider/network; account labels are used only for selection and are not sent to Groq. The summary instructions distinguish market-specific claims from incidental mentions, lifestyle anecdotes, fiction, advertisements, and promotions; when evidence does not establish a market thesis, the summary should say so. Any remaining social assertion is to be described as what a post says, not as an independently verified fact. One excerpt must be attributed as one post; plural language requires at least three distinct excerpts, which still does not establish author independence. Each AI claim must be one sentence and cite exact submitted evidence; each comparison claim must cite both evidence groups. Citation links under each overview passage jump to the cited records in the consolidated Sources register, including cited records outside its initial preview. Citation-ID validation does not prove semantic entailment or factual accuracy, so inspect the original records. If AI is unconfigured, unavailable, rate-limited, times out, or fails citation validation, Atlas shows a labeled evidence overview and the available failure category rather than inventing a synthesis. The privacy notice describes this processing; free-tier eligibility is not verified by the application.

The code does not verify provider billing status. The operator must confirm free-tier eligibility independently and keep the feature disabled if free access is not confirmed. No paid plan or billing details are required by Atlas. Groq's [data handling](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data) notes that prompts may be retained for reliability/abuse monitoring for up to 30 days; its [billing FAQ](https://console.groq.com/docs/billing-faqs) describes paid plan requirements.

## Interpretation

Returned counts are records, not unique people, public sentiment, prevalence, attention, investor positioning, or national opinion. Platform members, ranking systems, community languages, source caps, translations, and API indexing differ. Empty results do not establish absence. Verify material claims against primary information such as filings, official data, and company disclosures. Do not infer a financial conclusion from conversation volume alone.

The on-demand search is separate from protected scheduled ingestion, which may retain operational metadata in Supabase. See the [privacy notice](https://signal-scout-xi-ruby.vercel.app/privacy) and [source register](https://signal-scout-xi-ruby.vercel.app/sources).
