# Limitations

Atlas searches selected public indexes; it does not comprehensively scrape the web or provide a representative global sample. Coverage depends on user-entered phrases, provider ranking/indexing, languages, source activity, API limits, date windows, and current availability. One provider may return partial results while another fails or is intentionally not queried; source status is displayed separately.

Lexical matching cannot reliably resolve every ticker, alias, common word, translation, spelling variant, or paraphrase. Alternate phrases must be entered explicitly. Headline-only sources can omit body-only matches; short excerpts do not replace reading the source in context. Search summaries flag exact repeats and conservative long-text near-copies within a source network, but not semantic paraphrases, translations, or cross-network copying; common boilerplate can trigger a false match. Syndicated or repeated claims may therefore still appear across sources and require researcher review.

Language, platform, publisher, server, or community does not establish a contributor's location. Returned records are not unique people, population sentiment, investor attention, prevalence, or market behavior. Online conversation alone does not establish a company's demand, margins, financial materiality, causality, or valuation. Atlas does not issue investment recommendations.

When the operator enables Groq and its free usage is available, Atlas automatically requests a short AI synthesis of at most eight screened discussion excerpts per side; source links and author handles are excluded. Otherwise, or if the AI response fails Atlas's structural citation checks, the interface falls back to a deterministic evidence overview. Citation checks verify that claim IDs exist in the submitted evidence group, not that a claim is entailed by its excerpt; semantic claim accuracy remains unvalidated. For a named-language overview, only records with an understood, matching provider language label may enter the AI request. Atlas does not promote recurring n-grams as themes because generic language can recur without evidentiary value. Treat the interface as a discovery aid and verify consequential claims against original and primary sources. Provider requests time out after 12 seconds, but this does not increase source availability or completeness. Search-result pages do not save user query history; separate operational ingestion may retain historical metadata under its own controls.
